A RESEARCH STUDY ON CELLULAR GEOMETRY AND LOAD-BEARING COLUMN PERFORMANCE

Evaluation of Biomimetic Cellular Structures for the Strength-to-Weight Performance of Load-Bearing Columns

An experimental investigation of biomimetic cellular geometry in small-scale load-bearing column models.

Experimental methodologyResearch motivation
RESEARCH QUESTION“To what extent do biomimetic cellular structures improve the strength-to-weight performance of load-bearing column models compared with a conventional structural design?”

Overview

This research investigates whether structural geometries inspired by biological forms may influence the strength-to-weight performance of fabricated column specimens. The study involves CAD design, specimen fabrication, controlled mechanical testing, data collection, and comparative analysis.

Study objectives

  • Develop and fabricate column specimens incorporating selected biomimetic cellular geometries.
  • Establish a conventional structural design for comparative testing.
  • Evaluate mechanical performance under consistent testing conditions.
  • Record specimen mass and relevant mechanical performance measurements.
  • Analyze structural performance in relation to specimen mass.
  • Evaluate the extent to which geometry influences the measured performance within the limitations of the study.

Research Motivation

Engineering interests

I am interested in civil engineering, architecture, structural engineering, physics, and mathematics. What connects them for me is how the shape of a structure affects the way it behaves under load.

From a design to a physical structure

During an architecture internship, I worked with CAD and architectural design materials, including a house design, and I developed an architectural model. Through my Eagle Scout service project, I planned and led the construction of garden beds, signage, and a composting area for an elementary school garden. These were student projects, but together they showed me different parts of the process, from working with plans and models to considering how a design is translated into a physical structure.

Geometry as a variable

Those experiences raised questions about how the geometry of a structure influences its behavior. Rather than focusing only on the materials used in a structural component, I wanted to investigate how the arrangement of the material itself might influence mechanical performance.

Why cellular columns

Columns transfer loads, and their performance depends on geometry along with material, slenderness, and fabrication. That makes a column a reasonable component for testing geometry in a controlled, small-scale setting. Biological structures such as honeycomb, lobster shell, and sea urchin skeletons offer cellular arrangements to interpret. I do not assume they are better than a conventional design. The experiment tests whether they show measurable differences.

What I hope to learn

Through this research, I hope to develop a more thorough understanding of structural design, experimental engineering, and the relationship between geometry and mechanical performance.

Experimental Methodology

Process

STEP 1Literature ReviewBiomimicry, cellular structures, load-bearing columns, and strength-to-weight evaluation were reviewed to define the designs and the comparison.
STEP 2CAD DesignThe honeycomb-inspired, lobster shell-inspired, sea urchin-inspired, and conventional control columns are designed in CAD before fabrication.
STEP 3Specimen FabricationSpecimens are fabricated with consistent conditions to reduce variability between designs.
STEP 4Mechanical TestingEvery specimen is tested under the same testing conditions so results can be compared.
STEP 5Data CollectionSpecimen mass and the measured mechanical performance are recorded for each specimen.
STEP 6Comparative AnalysisPerformance is considered relative to specimen mass, and each biomimetic design is compared with the conventional control.

Experimental design

This is a comparative experiment with four specimen groups: honeycomb-inspired, lobster shell-inspired, sea urchin-inspired, and a conventional control. The control is the baseline. Any difference in strength-to-weight performance is judged against it.
